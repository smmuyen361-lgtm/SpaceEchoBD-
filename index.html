Forgotten but Not Lost

Stories of human-made equipment left behind on the Moon and Mars—and what these objects can teach us about exploration, engineering, and discovery.

Why look back?

Space exploration leaves more than footprints. Mission hardware, scientific instruments, and landing systems can remain after a mission ends. These objects tell stories about how people explored other worlds and how science is built from many missions over time.

This is an educational storytelling prototype. Check mission details against the linked NASA sources before using them in a final presentation.

Stories from other worlds

The Moon · Apollo

Lunar Module Descent Stages

Apollo Lunar Modules carried astronauts down to the lunar surface. The descent stages were left on the Moon when astronauts departed in the ascent stages.

Explore NASA history

The Moon · Science

Lunar Laser Ranging Retroreflectors

Apollo missions placed retroreflector arrays on the Moon. Scientists can bounce laser pulses off them to measure the Earth–Moon distance with high precision.

Explore NASA Moon science

Mars · Pathfinder

Pathfinder and Sojourner

NASA's Mars Pathfinder mission delivered the Sojourner rover to Mars in 1997. The lander and rover became part of the record of early mobile exploration on Mars.

A simple mission timeline

1969–1972 · Apollo Moon landings
Human crews explored the lunar surface and left mission hardware behind.
1971 onward · Lunar science instruments
Scientific equipment left on the Moon continued to support research, including laser ranging.
1997 · Mars Pathfinder
Sojourner demonstrated mobile robotic exploration on the Martian surface.

Our project

SpaceEcho BD shares the stories behind human-made objects left on the Moon and Mars. We aim to make planetary exploration approachable through concise storytelling, mission timelines, and links to trusted NASA resources.
